# Kin: the pitch

## One sentence

**Kin is a health companion for people far from care: it knows your medicines,
checks you when something feels off, and gets you ready for an emergency, with
or without signal.**

## Two sentences

In remote northern communities the nearest doctor can be a flight away and the
nursing station is often short-staffed. Kin turns the phone people already own
into the first step of care: it works in airplane mode, hands a clean note to
the nurse the moment signal returns, and shows the community an outbreak
forming before the clinic would have known.

## The problem

- Nursing stations in remote Ontario and Manitoba have reported vacancy rates
  above 60%. People fly south even for routine appointments.
- Help in an emergency can be far away. The people already in the room are the
  first responders, trained or not.
- Seniors on many prescriptions live far from a pharmacist. The same drug
  under two brand names is an easy and dangerous mistake.
- Most health apps stop working when the signal does.

## What Kin does

| Mode | What you do | What Kin does |
|---|---|---|
| **Medicine** | Show it your medicine cabinet | Reads each label, matches it to Health Canada's drug database, flags the same drug twice and risky pairs, builds your day |
| **Check** | Say what is wrong, take your pulse on the camera, record your cough, add a photo | Tells you how urgent it is and what to do now. Never a diagnosis |
| **Ready** | Practise CPR on a pillow | Measures your pace with the phone's motion sensor and coaches you to the right rhythm |
| **Emergency** | Press one button | Dials 911, shows your location to read out, shows the approved first-aid steps and your medical summary |
| **Clinic** | (the nurse's screen) | Notes arrive sorted by urgency. A community view shows clusters of illness and how many households have practised CPR |

## Why it is safe

One rule runs through the whole product: **the AI assists, the protocol
decides, a human is in charge.**

- Gemini listens, reads labels and writes the nurse's note. It never decides
  how urgent something is and never invents a first-aid step.
- Urgency comes from a fixed, written protocol that a clinical lead can read
  line by line. First-aid steps come from fixed scripts.
- Measurements come from sensors. Pulse and compression pace are computed,
  not guessed.
- With no signal, or if any AI step fails, Kin falls back to the protocol and
  the scripts, which live on the phone.

## Why it is a Google product through and through

- **Gemini API** (through Firebase AI Logic): understands what you say in your
  own words and language, reads medicine labels from the camera, describes a
  photo for the nurse, and writes the handoff note.
- **Gemini Nano on the device**: the same request runs on the phone when there
  is no signal, through Firebase AI Logic hybrid inference.
- **Cloud Firestore**: offline-first sync. A note written in airplane mode is
  delivered when signal returns, with no code path of our own.
- **Firebase Auth, Hosting and Security Rules**: anonymous by default, and
  only clinicians can read notes.
- **Material 3** design, Google Sans Flex and Material Symbols, with colour
  generated by Google's Material colour library.

## The 90-second demo

1. **Medicine.** Add Tylenol and a cold remedy. Kin says both contain
   acetaminophen and to ask a pharmacist.
2. **Airplane mode on.** Say "my chest hurts and I can't catch my breath."
   Kin answers at once: call 911, and why.
3. **Still offline.** Do a routine check with a pulse reading. The note shows
   "Waiting for signal".
4. **Ready.** A judge kneels at a pillow. Kin says "push faster" until they
   hit 100 to 120 a minute.
5. **Airplane mode off.** The note lands on the nurse's screen, sorted by
   urgency. The community view shows a respiratory cluster on the East Side.
